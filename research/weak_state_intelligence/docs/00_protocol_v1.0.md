# Why Do Weak States Face More Rebellion?

*A Research Protocol on Incumbent Intelligence, Bargaining Errors, and Civil-War Escalation*

**Research-assistant feasibility study · 14 October 2026 · Version 1.0**

**Abstract**

Weak state capacity is a familiar correlate of civil war, but “weakness” bundles distinct deficits and does not by itself identify the step that turns a political challenge into violence. This protocol tests a specific alternative to the usual coercive-capacity account: weak incumbents may systematically underestimate a challenger's military capability or local support, overestimate their own prospects, and make an initial offer below what could have prevented escalation. The research assistant's first assignment is a decision-oriented feasibility study, not a regression designed to confirm the claim. It specifies the eligible disputes, the dated evidence required to observe beliefs and offers, rival mechanisms, datasets, archival sampling, and precommitted criteria for a **GO** or **NO-GO** decision. A later substantive test may yield **YES**, **NO**, or **INDETERMINATE**; missing records cannot count as evidence that the theory is false.

## Question, motivation, and the decision this study must make

### The apparent truism is an unresolved causal question

The intuition that weak states are prone to rebellion runs through classic accounts of state formation and modern civil-war scholarship. [Fearon and Laitin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000534) gave the contemporary claim an especially influential form: low income proxies, in part, for a state's limited ability to deter or defeat an incipient insurgency. Precisely because the claim is familiar, it is easy to pass over its meaning. What, concretely, is weak: taxation, deployed coercive resources, administrative reach, intelligence about a specific adversary, or credible authority to implement a settlement? By what sequence does that weakness become war? Does it make a challenger more likely to organize, a government less likely to notice it, a negotiation more likely to fail, or a small violent campaign more likely to survive long enough to meet the conventional civil-war threshold? These are distinct transitions, not interchangeable versions of “onset.” The project begins by unpacking the apparent truism, not by assuming that one index of “state capacity” measures all its proposed mechanisms.

The temporal puzzle is particularly useful. A persistently weak state may have a higher *between-country* hazard of rebellion; a constant can explain differences across units. It cannot, on its own, explain why one challenger escalates this month while another in the same weak state does not. The timing of exposure to a challenger, information acquisition, offers, and changes in force must supply the within-country account. Recent work on state penetration and locally disruptive state-building explains some violence following expanded state reach ([Ying (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022002720957063)). It does not exhaust the problem of rebellion in states that remain chronically weak without a clear capacity-building shock. Our target is the latter question, while retaining state-building shocks as a separate mechanism rather than treating them as evidence for this one.

One bargaining observation makes the puzzle sharper. If both sides accurately know that the government is militarily weak, that weakness changes their expected division of the stakes, but does not necessarily eliminate the bargaining range. To generate war through *information*, the theory needs more than low precision: it needs a directional error in the incumbent's assessment, a resulting first offer, and a challenger response. We ask whether this sequence exists often enough to be an independent and substantively important explanation of the weak-state association, after allowing for coercive incapacity, commitment, reputation, and rebel miscalculation.

### Two estimands, two decisions

The **conditional bargaining question** is: among independently observed organized challengers before their first armed action, does the incumbent's information about that challenger affect its pre-decision estimate of relative strength or support, its first response (including no offer), and escalation within 24 months, conditional on pre-existing local coercive capacity and other relevant conditions? The primary unit is a government–challenger–month. “Observed” refers to the research record, not necessarily contemporaneous government awareness; awareness must be coded as an outcome-relevant state. The total escalation question uses all eligible claimants. The narrower belief–offer path can be evaluated only where a first decision and its prior information are actually observable, with selection into that subset reported explicitly.

The **population question** is: how much of the association between weak state capacity and country-level civil-war onset occurs at claimant formation/detection, bargaining failure/first violence, and persistence to the 25-battle-death threshold? Its initial country-year and movement-year analyses are stage accounting. They are not, without stronger identification, a causal decomposition or an estimate of the information channel's population share. A visible self-determination-claimant cohort answers only part of this population question.

The first deliverable is a binary **feasibility decision**: **GO** means the assistant has demonstrated that the necessary belief–offer–response evidence and relevant comparisons can be collected at scale; **NO-GO** means the strong mechanism claim is not identifiable with the assembled sources. A later substantive answer is **YES**, **NO**, or **INDETERMINATE**. The third outcome is mandatory when the data cannot distinguish the theory from its rivals. This separation gives the assistant a precise yes/no task now without promising an artificially precise causal verdict later.

## Where the argument sits in the literature

The starting point is not a vacant literature. [Fearon and Laitin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000534) emphasize conditions conducive to insurgency and the government's ability to find and suppress rebels; their discussion of poor local information is mainly about counterinsurgency, not a measured prewar estimate that determines a settlement offer. [Fearon (2010), pp. 40–42](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/119531468181479612/pdf/620090WP0Gover0BOX0361475B00PUBLIC0.pdf) explicitly asks why governments facing stronger opposition do not simply adjust their terms and proposes incomplete information, alongside commitment problems and opposition error. [Beardsley et al. (2017), p. 677](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007123415000307) explicitly suggests that an incumbent that underestimates the cost of conflict may offer too little. Their test concerns conflict-prevention intervention, not a directly observed sequence of incumbent belief, initial offer, and challenger response. The verbal information mechanism therefore cannot be claimed as new; its direct measurement and relative adjudication would be the contribution.

Other close work narrows that contribution further. [Cunningham (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12003) makes opposition fragmentation a source of uncertainty over which concession would suffice. [Bartusevičius and Gleditsch (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818318000425) and [Germann and Sambanis (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818320000557) already distinguish the formation of claims from escalation, so merely splitting stages is not novel. [Walter (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-5907.2006.00186.x) explains deliberate nonconcession through precedent for future separatists, and [Walter (2007)](https://connect.apsanet.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2017/06/2007-summer.pdf) explicitly links weak states to reputation and commitment. [Grigoryan (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1162/ISEC_a_00200) identifies concessions that may strengthen future separatism. [Thomas et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818316000175) shows that challengers can seek rejection through deliberately infeasible demands. [Malone and Spaniel (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1177/2053168021993971) supplies a theoretical counterexample: under specified uncertainty about fighting costs or power, changed valuations can induce safer proposer offers. It is not direct evidence about weak civil-war governments. These are live alternatives, not footnotes to the preferred mechanism.

The research frontier is therefore specific: *Do chronically weak incumbents, more than comparable better-informed incumbents, make optimistic prewar errors about a particular challenger, translate those errors into an insufficient feasible initial offer, and thereby increase escalation independently of coercive weakness?* The project must separately assess whether this pathway is prevalent enough to illuminate the broader weak-state correlation. A strong paper could also find that weakness works primarily through coercive incapacity or commitment; a well-measured negative result would still answer the question.

## Theory, falsifiers, and distinct observable implications

### Mechanism and scope conditions

Let the government keep share $x\in[0,1]$ of a divisible political stake. The government estimates its probability of prevailing in conflict as $p_G$ and bears war cost $c_G$; the challenger evaluates government victory at $p_R$ and bears cost $c_R$, in comparable utility units. The government's minimum acceptable share is $p_G-c_G$; the challenger's maximum acceptable government share is $p_R+c_R$. A subjective bargaining range requires

$$

p_G-p_R \le c_G+c_R.

$$

If common knowledge of weakness moves both $p_G$ and $p_R$ downward together, the range may simply move. A government that *overestimates* its position relative to the challenger can instead demand too much. This stylized model does not rule out commitment, nondivisible issues, or strategic reputation; it isolates what the information explanation must show.

The proposed chain has four distinct links: (1) weak challenger-specific collection or analysis, measured from the government's *inputs* before its first decision (informant or field-report coverage, reporting latency, corroboration, and whether a responsible office received the assessment); (2) an optimistic incumbent forecast of the challenger's capability, support, or the government's victory prospects relative to a triangulated, independently dated benchmark; (3) a first feasible offer less generous than one the challenger would have accepted; and (4) refusal and subsequent escalation. Input quality must be coded independently of forecast accuracy and later violence, or the first link becomes tautological. The benchmark itself is uncertain: compare multiple contemporaneous independent sources on the same pre-existing construct, record their range, and classify directional error only when the government's assessment meaningfully lies outside it. Both military capability and local support can affect the offer, but they must be recorded separately. The phrase “weak state” is a condition, not a substitute for measuring any link. The full weak-state argument further requires a measured association from *baseline weakness* to worse challenger-specific inputs and optimistic error; a chain found equally in strong states would not explain the classic correlation.

The main hypotheses and their hard tests are:

- **H1.** **Directional beliefs.** At a comparable independently assessed threat, lower challenger-specific input quality predicts more optimistic *private, pre-decision* government assessments. Low precision alone predicts no direction. Calibrated or systematically cautious weak incumbents contradict the strong version.

- **H2.** **Offer transmission.** At comparable military resources, demands, bargaining authority, and commitment conditions, a more optimistic assessment precedes a materially less generous first offer or an explicit refusal to discuss feasible concessions. A rejected offer alone is not evidence of insufficiency; the challenger's contemporaneous acceptable terms must be investigated.

- **H3.** **Escalation.** The erroneous assessment and offer precede first organized armed action within 24 months; improved information that corrects beliefs should sometimes lead to revised terms before military resources or demands change. If credible corrections alter beliefs but not feasible offers, the proposed offer channel loses support.

- **H4.** **Macro relevance.** The pathway appears across both escalating and non-escalating disputes and, with validated coverage, explains a meaningful part of the weak-state association in a specified claimant universe. Case traces alone cannot establish this hypothesis.

### Discrimination against competing explanations

**Rival mechanisms and discriminating observations**

- **Military weakness.** Before violence: Government recognizes the threat but lacks local forces, reach, or resources to deter or defeat it; violence may persist to threshold. Discriminating evidence: Accurate internal threat assessment and realistic offer, followed by inability to enforce order or suppress attacks. Record local rather than merely national forces.

- **Reputation.** Before violence: Government understands current challenger but intentionally withholds concessions to deter other challengers. Discriminating evidence: Private discussion of a feasible present settlement rejected because of expected future claims; no offer revision after threat information improves.

- **Commitment.** Before violence: A deal is known but cannot be enforced, or concession raises the challenger's future power. Discriminating evidence: Specific enforcement objection or documented expectation of future empowerment despite accurate current assessment; credible guarantee should change behavior.

- **Rebel strategy.** Before violence: Challenger overestimates its own prospects or deliberately asks for unattainable terms. Discriminating evidence: Incumbent assessment is calibrated; private challenger/mediator records place the minimum acceptable terms above any feasible government offer.

- **Cautious offers.** Before violence: Poor information leads to generous or delayed offers rather than arrogance. Discriminating evidence: Wide internal uncertainty intervals and a comparatively generous first offer at the same benchmarked threat.

- **State penetration.** Before violence: A recent intervention changes local rights, extraction, or customary order, creating new opposition. Discriminating evidence: Timing and location of mobilization follow state expansion; no prior comparable challenger and no belief–offer error needed.

An information revelation is unusually informative only when it describes *pre-existing* challenger capability or support and precedes a belief revision, then an offer revision, without a simultaneous material change in deployed forces, rebel preferences, guarantees, or precedent concerns. Elections, censuses, mediators, roads, and third-party reports can carry information, but often also change mobilization, pressure, or enforcement. None is a valid instrument merely because it is labeled an “information shock.”

## Evidence plan

### Comparative archival pilot: cases and process evidence

The initial risk set is the public Self-Determination Movements (SDM) universe, which includes many never-violent claims ([Sambanis et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022002717735364)). The assistant will construct all eligible movement–incumbent–years from first independently recorded organized demand until first anti-state violence, claim termination, or right-censoring. A monthly archival file begins at first demand where dates permit. The initial analysis concerns the *explicit self-determination claim pathway*. It must not be called a universal sample of civil-war origins: peripheral armed groups can be hidden before overt violence ([Lewis and Rangazas (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1537592725102065)), and the Armed Group Dataset begins after an armed group has already formed ([Malone (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/isq/sqac050)).

For feasibility, draw **24 disputes before inspecting archive richness**. Stratify prospectively by pre-dispute higher/lower public information-capacity proxy and higher/lower local coercive capacity, with escalating and non-escalating disputes represented in each feasible cell. These quotas audit document yield, not population frequency. Record the entire screened pool, sampling probabilities, unavailable cases, and sources searched. Never substitute famous wars for inaccessible nonwars without recording the substitution. Do not use the deliberately balanced pilot unweighted to estimate an escalation rate. Include at least one disagreement case in which information looks poor yet bargaining succeeds, and one in which government information looks good yet escalation occurs, if the prespecified frame contains them.

Each candidate must be traced in this order: first independent claim; date and basis of government awareness; pre-decision intelligence inputs and first private threat assessment, if any; at least two independent contemporaneous assessments of the *same* pre-existing capability or support construct, or a justified benchmark interval; initial substantive offer, documented decision to refuse talks, or no observed response; challenger response where there was a communicable government decision, and contemporaneous evidence of acceptable terms; any new information; subsequent belief/offer revisions; first anti-state violent act or verified absence of one during 24 months of follow-up; any government-initiated violence/repression; and eventual UCDP threshold, if any. For each document capture date, author, intended audience, quotation, archive locator, contemporaneity, and coder confidence. Public threats are not automatically private beliefs; later military defeat is not a prewar forecast benchmark. Disagreement with a single third-party estimate is not automatically misperception. An absence of a found offer is `unknown`, not `no_offer`. A government may lack a negotiator's authority even if it has accurate information; code authority separately.

The minimum *complete offer chain* is dated pre-decision intelligence inputs; a private government estimate; a triangulated contemporaneous comparator with uncertainty; an *actual dated first offer with terms*; a dated challenger response; credible evidence of feasible acceptance terms; and a dated first violent act or documented violence-free observation through 24 months. Explicit refusal is a separate branch, useful for studying nonconcession but *not* counted as an observed offer chain. No observed government response is another branch. Without a credible acceptance benchmark, code “offer rejected, reason unknown.” The assistant will provide two full example records, one escalating and one non-escalating, plus at least one failed or missing-record example; the principal investigator must be able to reproduce every coding judgment from the cited source.

### Quantitative risk set and the limits of available data

The public SDM dataset covers self-determination movements during 1945–2012, including more than two thirds that never escalated to violence. Detailed government responses exist for a random subsample, not all movements; its coded accommodations are not a global file of first rejected offers ([Sambanis et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022002717735364)). The updated Strategies of Resistance Data Project (SRDP) provides organization-year tactics and demands for 1960–2020, but its organizations are nested within a narrower movement universe, so it is an auxiliary timing source rather than an interchangeable denominator ([Cunningham et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/00223433241267866)). UCDP Armed Conflict and Dyadic 26.1 cover 1946–2025; the dedicated intrastate onset file is 25.1 and ends in 2024. A UCDP state-based conflict reaches 25 battle-related deaths in a calendar year; that threshold is neither necessarily the first armed act nor a pure bargaining outcome ([UCDP (2026)](https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/)).

The assistant will freeze versions and create a reviewed movement–organization–UCDP actor/dyad crosswalk with IDs, aliases, dates, and match confidence. No country–year name join is acceptable as an automatic actor match. Before combining ACD/Dyadic 26.1 with the onset 25.1 file, compare conflict and dyad IDs and onset dates in the overlapping years; reconcile discrepancies or derive a consistently defined onset series from 26.1. Restrict the overlap analysis to 1960–2012 and a separately labeled post-Cold-War descriptive window, 1990–2012. The announced SDM 2.0 extension to 2020 is not yet public on its author page; request access as an option, but the baseline may not depend on receiving it ([Self-Determination Movements 2.0 (2026)](https://michagermann.github.io/data/sdm2/)). PA-X catalogs concluded agreements, not the rejected first offers needed here ([PA-X (2026)](https://www.peaceagreements.org/downloads/)). The Information Capacity Index measures census, civil registration, and statistical infrastructure, not intelligence about a named rebel group ([Brambor et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0010414019843432)). It may stratify a pilot or test an upstream association only after validation against archival input quality and belief accuracy.

Within the claim risk set, report by capacity stratum: claimant entry; documented government awareness; first substantive offer, explicit refusal, or unobserved response; first armed act; and crossing of the UCDP threshold. Use competing risks and censoring conventions appropriate to claim termination and observation end. The country-year analysis separately describes the weak/strong capacity onset gap, with 0-war years retained, and asks how much of that *descriptive* gap could plausibly be located in each observed transition. Do not call the relative sizes of regression coefficients “relative causal contributions.”

### Identification strategy and what would invalidate it

The main comparison is among challengers with similar independently measured pre-dispute strength, demands, local government forces, and opportunities for settlement, but different quality of incumbent information *before* its first decision. Code information quality from dated collection inputs and reporting channels, independently of the incumbent's resulting belief error. The hypothesized treatment is challenger-specific information quality, not generic GDP or an undifferentiated state-capacity index. To connect the result back to the weak-state paradigm, predefine baseline weakness using lagged log income per capita for comparison with [Fearon and Laitin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000534), then report separately lagged extraction, administration, and locally deployable coercion; test whether these baselines predict the information inputs and optimistic error rather than treating them as synonyms. Outcome clocks start with an independently observed demand, not with UCDP onset or the first negotiation. Government awareness is an intermediate state; selecting only cases the incumbent noticed would select on the proposed treatment. Lagged national administrative and coercive measures will be kept separate, but conditioning on a post-threat troop deployment may block part of the causal pathway or introduce bias. The assistant must draw and date the causal graph before fitting a model.

Three analysis layers have different evidentiary status. First, a full-frame transition analysis establishes where the weak-state association appears, while transparently modeling censoring and measurement. Second, the archival pilot validates or rejects the ability to measure directional government error and links it to offers. Third, if feasibility gates pass, a prespecified comparative study can estimate the information–belief and belief–offer associations with country/period controls or matched risk sets, and use information revelations for within-episode process tests. A causal claim about escalation additionally requires defending exchangeability or a genuinely isolated information shock. We currently have *no verified instrument or discontinuity* that shifts government knowledge without also changing mobilization, bargaining pressure, coercion, or enforceability. Mediator arrival, elections, roads, and censuses are therefore candidate process observations, not identification shortcuts.

Positivity is a hard gate: there must be comparable episodes that combine low information with high force, and high information with low force, at similar threat levels. Plot the joint support of information and coercion before estimating separate effects. Archival preservation may be better in stronger states, and war episodes may generate more records than peaceful ones; track these selection probabilities. The 24-case audit can *screen* for these problems, not prove that missingness is ignorable or that overlap and power are adequate in a full study. Use the full screened frame and explicit selection bounds; show whether plausible missing assessments could reverse the sign. If documented challengers are systematically absent from weak-state peripheries, treat generalization to all onset as unresolved, even if conditional bargaining results are strong.

## Execution, decision rules, and handoff

### Five-week active-work sequence

1. **Week 1:** **Freeze the theory and sources.** Deliver a two-page literature boundary memo distinguishing [Fearon and Laitin (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000534), [Fearon (2010)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/119531468181479612/pdf/620090WP0Gover0BOX0361475B00PUBLIC0.pdf), [Beardsley et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007123415000307), reputation, commitment, fragmentation, rebel error, and stage-selection predecessors; a source registry with DOI/URL/page or table; a signed construct dictionary; and a predeclared smallest effect that would matter if full estimation becomes possible. Do not inspect pilot outcomes before freezing sampling and coding rules.

2. **Week 2:** **Build the denominator.** Download public SDM and relevant documentation; freeze UCDP 26.1 and onset 25.1 versions; optionally SRDP for tactic dates. Produce the full claim-risk-set flow, year coverage, movement and country counts, right-censoring rules, and an actor-linkage ledger. Report the size and direction of any stage-specific weak-state association, but label it descriptive.

3. **Week 3:** **Screen archival availability.** Draw 24 prespecified disputes. Search government, challenger, and third-party archives with a logged query protocol. Record availability for *all* selected disputes, including nonwars and empty searches. Produce three pilot coding examples and a blind second-coder review of the ambiguous elements.

4. **Week 4:** **Code and attack the mechanism.** Build dated belief–benchmark–offer–response traces. Try to disprove the favored mechanism using accurate-but-hardline governments, belief corrections without offer revisions, cautious weak governments, unimplementable bargains, and rebel overdemands. Run joint-support and missingness audits; revise only coding errors, not hypotheses after seeing the pattern.

5. **Week 5:** **Issue the decision memo.** Deliver a one-page **GO**/**NO-GO** ruling with gate-by-gate evidence and a reproducible package. If **GO**, supply a full-study sample-size/power calculation, prespecified comparison, collection budget, and strongest remaining identification threat. If **NO-GO**, state exactly which link failed and what newly accessible source would reopen it.

These are active-work weeks; external archive permissions and an optional SDM 2.0 request have separate wait times. The assistant must not delay the public-data feasibility audit while waiting for them.

### Precommitted binary feasibility gate

Issue **GO** only if *all* of the following are demonstrated and documented: (i) an eligible previolence claimant denominator including non-escalating disputes; (ii) at least 12 of the 24 prespecified pilot disputes with a complete pre-outcome information-input–belief–triangulated-benchmark–*actual first offer*–response–follow-up chain, including at least four escalating and four non-escalating disputes across at least four countries (refusals are coded separately and do not count toward the 12); (iii) an initial screen showing cross-classified overlap of information and coercive capacity at comparable threat and a recoverable baseline weakness measure in both information strata; (iv) a full-screened-frame archive-availability ledger and selection bounds that do not make the proposed sign wholly contingent on outcome-selective preservation; and (v) a feasible full-study collection and power plan for a prospectively fixed minimum meaningful contrast. The numeric pilot thresholds are *management criteria for continuation*, not statistical proof that missingness is ignorable, that overlap is adequate, or that publication is likely. If any gate fails, issue **NO-GO** for the *strong belief–offer–escalation study* and identify any narrower descriptive study that remains possible. A failure of access or comparability is not a substantive refutation.

For a full substantive test after **GO**, use three locked labels. Define $\Delta=P(Y_{24}=1\mid\text{low information})-P(Y_{24}=1\mid\text{high information})$ in the prespecified common-support claimant population, under a defended causal design, and a prospectively fixed minimum meaningful risk difference $\delta^*>0$. **YES** requires replicated dated optimistic incumbent errors preceding less-generous feasible offers, evidence that baseline weakness predicts poorer inputs and such errors independently of force, informative corrections that change offers before relevant force/commitment conditions change, credible comparisons including nonwars, and a confidence interval for identified $\Delta$ lying wholly above $\delta^*$. **NO** to a *substantively important* channel requires valid measurement and sufficient precision with the upper confidence bound below $\delta^*$; calibrated/cautious weak incumbents, absent offer transmission, and accurate deliberate refusals strengthen that verdict. **INDETERMINATE** applies when identification, beliefs, feasible rejected offers, counterfactuals, overlap, temporal separation, or precision is inadequate. A nonsignificant coefficient on a national information index does *not* by itself warrant **NO**.

### Required submission package

The assistant will submit: `source_registry.csv`; `risk_set_flow.csv` and documented construction code; `movement_actor_crosswalk.csv`; `pilot_sampling.csv` with original selection and replacements; `archival_search_log.csv`; `belief_offer_event_ledger.csv`; full citations or scans permitted by archive access; a codebook; at least three auditable case traces; overlap and missingness figures; a rival-mechanism matrix; and `decision_memo.pdf`. Every empirical assertion in the memo must point to a row, a document, or a cited publication. If no original-data analysis is possible in Week 2, report that fact rather than filling the gap with invented numbers.

The principal investigator's acceptance check is simple: another reader should be able to reconstruct why each case entered the denominator, what the government appeared to know *before* its offer, which contemporaneous evidence makes that assessment inaccurate, what was offered, why the challenger could or could not accept, and why reputation, commitment, and military incapacity do not already explain the outcome. If these questions cannot be answered for a comparison set that includes nonwars, the appropriate deliverable is a precise **NO-GO** decision, not a suggestive causal claim.

## Appendix A. Formal decision logic and statistical criteria

Define $I_{it}$ as challenger-specific incumbent information quality from input channels *before the first government decision*, $K_{it}$ as locally deployable coercive capacity, $B_{it}$ as the incumbent's private assessment, $[T^L_{it},T^U_{it}]$ as a triangulated contemporaneous threat-benchmark interval, $O_{it}$ as actual first offer terms when observed, and $Y_{i,t:t+24}$ as first anti-state violence in the ensuing 24 months. Directional optimism is coded only if $B_{it}$ lies meaningfully on the government-favoring side of the benchmark interval after mapping both to the same ordered concept. If $B_{it}$ lies within that interval, or sources are not independent, classify the comparison as calibrated/uncertain rather than force an error score. Ordinal coding requires a blind, written mapping; do not pretend that an ordinal difference is a cardinal probability error. A direct chain needs $I\rightarrow B\rightarrow O\rightarrow Y$, with rival paths from $K$, reputation $R$, commitment $C$, challenger strategy $Q$, and state-building exposure $S$ to offers and outcomes. The offer path is observed in a selected subset; the total first-violence question retains every eligible claimant. Within-dispute revelation tests additionally require no material intervening change in $K,R,C,Q,S$.

The primary bargaining estimand, if defensible, is a 24-month first-violence risk contrast for a specified shift from low to high *validated* challenger-specific information among the previolence claimant population at common support in $K$ and threat. The offer-mediated component is *not* identified by putting $O$ in a regression: information may affect awareness, repression, and mobilization, while unmeasured preferences affect both offer and escalation. The assistant must draw a DAG, report total and conditional associations separately, and state every assumption needed for a mediated causal estimate. The macro association is a separate country-year estimand; it must not be inferred by multiplying case-study effect sizes.

Before any full-study outcome analysis, freeze the smallest substantively important 24-month risk difference $\delta^*$ and the level of precision needed to rule it in or out. The principal investigator should choose this threshold using the *full risk-set* baseline rate and theoretical relevance, never the deliberately outcome-balanced 24-case pilot and never the information–outcome coefficient; then simulate required sample size under clustering by country and movement. Report interval estimates, one-sided bounds for **NO**, sensitivity to archive-selection and unmeasured confounding, and exact analysis population. If required sample size exceeds the recoverable belief/offer records, the appropriate feasibility answer is **NO-GO**. The pilot's 12-case minimum is not a power calculation.

## Appendix B. Data construction and coding instructions

### State machine and mutually exclusive missingness

Record dated transitions through five separate stages: (1) independently observed organized claim; (2) government awareness, non-awareness, or unknown awareness; (3) first substantive offer, explicit refusal, verified no offer, or unknown response; (4) challenger acceptance, rejection, or unknown response where applicable; and (5) first anti-state violence, government-initiated violence, or verified absence of violence through 24 months. Record later UCDP threshold crossing or cessation separately. Do not force every case through negotiation. Preserve unknown, not observed, not applicable, no offer, zero concession, and right-censoring as distinct coded values. A documented absence of talks may establish no offer; failure to find talks does not.

### Minimum archival ledger fields

- **Identity/time:** Movement, organization, state, issue, episode ID; event/document date; claim date; government awareness date; first decision/offer date; challenger response date; first anti-state armed action or government-initiated violence date; 24-month follow-up date and source; access date.

- **Information inputs:** Pre-decision field-report coverage, informant network, reporting latency, corroboration, and receipt by decision-makers. Code each from contemporaneous sources *without using* the later forecast error or conflict outcome.

- **Belief and benchmark:** Exact private incumbent words about challenger manpower, arms, territorial reach, local support, expected war duration/cost, or victory odds; at least two independent contemporaneous estimates of the *same pre-existing* construct, or a justified benchmark interval; each source's uncertainty, document audience, contemporaneity, and coder confidence.

- **Offer and feasibility:** Specific initial terms in the relevant issue-space, who had authority to offer them, whether terms were implementable, challenger reply and reasons, private or mediator evidence of minimum acceptable terms; do not infer reservation value from rejection alone.

- **Capacity and rivals:** Local deployable state forces and logistics *before* offer; separate national fiscal, coercive, administrative, and statistical-information proxies; other active challengers; explicit precedent concerns; expected future power shift; enforcement guarantees; rebel outside assistance; intervening changes in each.

- **Document audit:** Archive/repository, collection, file and page, author, recipient, original language, exact quote, transcription or translation status, access restrictions, search query, coder, second-coder adjudication, and reason for missingness.

### Source and linkage discipline

Download only public files needed for the first gate. Record URL, DOI/version, download date, original filename, SHA-256, unit, temporal coverage, and terms of use in a source registry. Verify SDM's government-response sampling and weights against its documentation. Build a movement–organization–actor/dyad crosswalk with human-reviewed aliases and chronology; do not propagate a match across years before an organization existed. SDM2EPR and ACD2EPR use different source vintages and may assist but do not constitute an automatic harmonized crosswalk. Preserve unmatched and ambiguous entities. For reproducibility, raw source files are immutable; transformations and inclusion decisions live in scripts and ledgers.
